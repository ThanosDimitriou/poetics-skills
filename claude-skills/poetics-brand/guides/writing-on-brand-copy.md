# Writing on-brand copy

A practical companion to the "Voice & tone" section of `brand.md` — for a person or an AI
drafting actual sentences, not just knowing the rules.

## The two-voice system, in practice

Poetics copy uses two registers, matched to the two display typefaces:

**Narrator voice** (sans, most of your writing) — direct, factual, scannable. Use for services
copy, process descriptions, FAQ, case studies, About content.

> "We install single-seat VR pods. Everything will be delivered, cabled, and preloaded for
> instant use."

**Inner voice** (serif italic, used sparingly) — second person, present tense, sensory. Reserve
for genuinely experiential moments — describing what someone feels inside the experience, not
routine information.

> "You sit down. The lights dim. The room disappears."

Don't reach for inner voice by default — it's a special-occasion tool. If you're writing a
services page or an FAQ answer, you're in narrator voice the whole way through.

## A short checklist before you ship copy

- **Concrete, not abstract.** Would "transforms how people experience culture" survive being
  replaced with an actual sensory or factual detail? If yes, replace it.
- **No invented facts.** No made-up stats, dates, attendance numbers, or quotes. If you don't
  have a real number, say so or leave a `[PLACEHOLDER: ...]` marker rather than estimating.
- **No named films.** Never reference a specific film, franchise, or title — describe the program
  by theme only (art, documentary, performance, education). This is a hard brand rule, not a
  style preference.
- **Restraint check.** Read it back — are you using more than one emphasis device (italic phrase,
  mono label, exclamation point) in the same short stretch of text? Cut to one.
- **No hype words.** If a sentence would work equally well in a generic SaaS landing page, it's
  probably too abstract for Poetics. Ban list: "revolutionize," "seamless," "empowering,"
  "game-changing," "elevate," "10x," "disruptive."

## Examples: before / after

**Before:** "Poetics is revolutionizing how audiences experience culture with cutting-edge
immersive technology."
**After:** "Poetics installs pop-up immersive cinemas — single-seat VR pods with curated films —
in museums, festivals, and event spaces."
*(Why: concrete over abstract, no hype words, factual.)*

**Before:** "Our amazing team will handle everything so you don't have to worry about a thing!!"
**After:** "The team handles curation, installation, operation, and hygiene."
*(Why: no exclamation points in product copy per brand.md, direct and factual.)*

**Before (misusing inner voice everywhere):** "*You feel* the wonder. *You feel* the presence.
*You feel* changed."
**After:** "You sit down. The lights dim. The room disappears." — then switch back to narrator
voice for the next section, rather than stretching the italic device across an entire page.

## When you're not sure

If a piece of copy could go either way, default to narrator voice — it's the safer, more
frequently correct choice. Inner voice is the exception, used once or twice per document/page at
most (per brand.md's general restraint principle).
