# Writing on-brand copy

The one voice guide for everything Poetics writes: decks, proposals, documents, social posts,
emails. It is the website's voice (poeticsvr.com), so every piece sounds like the same company.
`guides/deck-copy.md` adds what's specific to slides; `guides/anti-slop.md` lists what gives copy
away as machine-written.

## The voice

From the website's brief:

> Plain, concrete, in the first person plural. Short sentences. Active verbs. The company is
> confident about operations and modest about art. Describe what happens, never how it feels:
> the photography carries the feeling.

- **We and you.** "We install it in a day." "You give us the room."
- **What happens, not how it feels.** "Pods arrive cabled and ready to play." Not "a seamless,
  unforgettable journey".
- **Confident about operations, modest about art.** State logistics flatly; don't sell the films
  with adjectives.
- **The smallest familiar word.** "Use", not "leverage". No metaphors.
- **Say it the way you would across a table.** If you wouldn't say it to a museum director, don't
  write it.
- **Say less.** One idea per paragraph, slide or post. Cut every word that doesn't change the
  meaning.

There is one voice. The old "inner voice" (second person, serif italic, "You sit down. The lights
dim.") is retired: the photographs carry the feeling.

## Headings

Website headings are short plain sentences with a full stop: "We run it ourselves.", "Tell us
about your space.", "What we have done so far.", "Why the name." Use the same everywhere.

- One short sentence, or a label when that's enough ("Price.").
- A full stop. Never a question, never a colon-subtitle, never wordplay.
- Sentence case.

## Mechanics

- **No em dashes.** A colon, a comma, a full stop or parentheses. En dashes in ranges (10–50).
- Sentence case everywhere, including buttons and labels. American spelling ("program",
  "organization").
- No exclamation marks, no emoji, no capitals for emphasis, no letter-spaced labels.
- A button or link names what happens: "Write to us", "Book a call".
- The only email is hello@poeticsvr.com. Don't name the people who made the material unless asked.

## Numbers and facts

- **Never invent** a figure, date, quote, name, partner or photograph. If you don't have it, write
  a `[PLACEHOLDER]` and say so.
- Every figure carries its as-of date once ("As of September 2026"). Round numbers keep their
  qualifier (5,000+). Don't repeat a figure twice in one piece. Don't turn a rating into a
  percentage.
- **Films:** never name or show a specific film, except in a curation deck made for one venue.
  Describe a program by theme (arts, education, entertainment).
- **Confidential** until Poetics clears it: pricing amounts, the franchise model, roadmap dates
  (other than the December festival), the streaming platform's status, internal tooling, survey
  methodology.

## Words that give it away

The website's banned list: journey, seamless, unforgettable, magical, cutting-edge, revolutionary,
solution, leverage, unlock, next-generation, elevate, transform your space. Also: delve, empower,
game-changing, world-class, bespoke, hassle-free, "where X meets Y", "step into", "like never
before", "it's not just X, it's Y", "ready when you are", "let's", "here's". Full list and why:
`guides/anti-slop.md`.

## Lines you can reuse

Approved, already in the voice:

- Immersive cinema, anywhere.
- You give us the room. We install the cinema.
- We review hundreds of titles and choose a handful for your audience. A program runs 10–50
  minutes.
- Each pod is one seat: a swivel chair, a headset and noise-cancelling headphones.
- Pods arrive cabled and ready to play.
- Trained operators run the room and control the equipment through Poetics Pods, our own software.
- We install it in a day and it is gone the next morning.
- Tell us about your space.

## Before you ship

- Would a sentence survive in a generic tech landing page? Then it's too abstract: replace it with
  what actually happens.
- Any em dash, exclamation mark or stock phrase? Remove it.
- Any number without a source and a date? Check it or mark it.
- Read the headings alone: do they say what each part is about, in plain words?
